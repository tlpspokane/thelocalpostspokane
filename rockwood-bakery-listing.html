A Century of Community — Featured in Edible Inland Northwest

The building at 315 E 18th Ave was built in 1912 as Rockwood Market, a neighborhood grocery store serving South Hill families when the neighborhood was just coming into its own. Over a century later, the same building now houses Rockwood Bakery — still a community hub, still serving the neighbors who walk down the street from Manito Park, still buzzing with the rhythm of everyday Spokane life. Edible Inland Northwest called it "a bustling community hub, always abuzz with activity."

About This Opportunity

Rockwood Bakery is one of Spokane's most beloved institutions — a South Hill community anchor with a 4.7-star rating across 707 reviews, a 100+ year history in the same building, and a loyal neighborhood following that fills it every Tuesday through Saturday. Just a short walk from Manito Park, it draws both dedicated regulars and visitors making a morning of it.

What sets this placement apart is the dwell time and the mood. Rockwood isn't a grab-and-go coffee shop. Customers sit down with their cinnamon rolls and lattes, open their laptops or journals, and stay. Reviewers describe it as the perfect place to "slow down and savor the familiar." An ad here isn't glimpsed in passing — it's seen in full, by someone relaxed and lingering.

The handwritten menus and warm plank flooring give Rockwood a particular character: it's a place that values the personal and the local. That same sensibility applies to the businesses customers want to discover here. A thoughtful placement at Rockwood reaches South Hill's most community-rooted, local-first adults at their most receptive.
